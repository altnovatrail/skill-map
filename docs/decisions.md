## The kind of the devcontainer and the browsers of the tests

**R02. The devcontainer of `skill-map` is of the template's kind `typescript`: the Biome extension and Biome as the formatter of TypeScript, and in the stage `toolchain` the three browsers of the tests — Chrome Headless Shell, WebKit and Firefox — from the image of Playwright, with their system packages from the snapshot of the Ubuntu archive. The browsers are named exceptions to the rule of licenses, and this entry is the decision that brings them in.** The kind and its three browsers come with the template of `spec`.
- **The exceptions** to the rule of licenses: Chrome Headless Shell, a build of Google Chrome for Testing, is on Google's free terms; WebKit is under LGPL-2.0-or-later and BSD-2-Clause, Firefox under MPL-2.0 (sources 7.7 spec). Each is a tool of the tests that never ships: NovaTrail distributes none of them — the stage `toolchain` is built in CI and in the devcontainer and published nowhere —, and `skill-map` and the platform are built, run and deployed without them. They are no npm packages, so `licenses.json` does not name them.
- **What runs in them** — the element's tests, Chromium with the coverage and WebKit and Firefox without it, and axe-core, an exception of its own. `playwright` in `package.json` is the image's version.
- **Biome** is the linter of the code too, so the kind's extension and setting stay as the template has them.

— *Why:* the container of `skill-map` is spec's with its kind: the element's tests need a browser, and the kind brings it pinned by the image's digest, with its system libraries from the snapshot. A difference from the template would be a decision of its own, and `.devcontainer/` is the template's output.

— *Rejected:* a devcontainer without the kind's stage while there was no code, or without WebKit and Firefox until their tests ran: `.devcontainer/` would differ from the template for no reason, and the difference would have to be a decision.

— *Consequence:* `.devcontainer/`; [architecture.md](architecture.md).

## The data and the element of the map

**R03. The package draws what `api` gives for the map by the contract, and it lays out the graph on the device itself: it needs no fields beyond the contract. The state on the map of a skill with a verdict it derives from the graph and the answer of `GetSkillMap` — there are five of them —, and a skill with a rubric or one that is not assessed has a mark instead of a state. The map is of one module, with the anchor skills before tier 1, and it has two views: the learner's and the adult's. Only a skill that is not locked can be chosen on it. The package holds no learner's data on the device.** How it is built is in [architecture.md](architecture.md).
- **The layout** is the package's, by the open view of the graph and the tiers `skill_tiers`, and it is the same on the same data. The contract has no coordinates.
- **The states on the map** of a skill whose assessability is `verdict` follow its `SkillState` and the graph's prerequisites, the first that fits, in order:
  1. "time to review" — `mastery_band` is not empty, and `review_due`, or `review_date` no later than the time of the device;
  2. "mastered" — `mastery_band` is not empty;
  3. "in progress" — `evidence_count` is greater than zero;
  4. "ready to start" — every prerequisite of the skill among the module's skills whose assessability is `verdict` has a `mastery_band` that is not empty; for a skill without such prerequisites, at once;
  5. "locked" — the rest.

  A mastered prerequisite that it is time to review does not lock the skill: time does not take away "mastered". A prerequisite with a rubric or not assessed, and an anchor skill of the skill, do not lock it either: the engine keeps the author's order, and whether to hold back a lesson on "not held" is for its rule to decide. The package reads a skill with a verdict that the answer does not name as a skill without evidence, and if the answer names a skill or an anchor skill twice, the first record counts. A skill with evidence — "in progress", "mastered" or "time to review" — is open even when its prerequisite is not mastered yet or has already lost "mastered": the learner has already worked on it. "Ready to start" rests on the same as the reason code "a new skill", `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL` — «пререквизиты освоены или их нет» ("the prerequisites are mastered, or there are none") in [the text of the contracts](../spec/contracts/README.md) —, but takes of them only the module's prerequisites with a verdict: the map does not see whether the others are mastered. The engine takes the map's rule as an input: for a skill that the map opened and the learner chose, `ChooseNext` gives a lesson or a task on it with the code "the learner chose the skill", if the pool and the lessons hold at least one — such a choice is empty only when `api` has limited the pool to tasks without AI —, and the reachability for "My subjects" may be its own. The package does not compute mastery: it matches the ready marks of `learner-model` against the graph. The rules of the states do not need the tiers `skill_tiers`: they are an input of the layout.
- **Rubric and not assessed.** A skill whose assessability is `rubric` or `none` stands in its tier, with its links, and instead of a state it has a mark — "feedback by criteria" or "not assessed": it has no state in `GetSkillMap`, and it is never "mastered". An adult sees the marks of the criteria in the progress of `app`, not on the map.
- **The anchor skills** that are the module's prerequisites stand before tier 1 — they have no tier of their own —, each with its `AnchorSkillState`: "held", "not held" or "unknown", with a reason. An anchor skill that the graph names and the answer does not is "unknown", without a reason. A value that the build does not know is "unknown", and a reason that it does not know is not shown (`progress.proto` of `spec`); a reason is shown only if it fits the holding — "not mastered" and "no evidence" with "not held", "nobody teaches" and "taught only without a verdict" with "unknown", and "held" has none. The title of an anchor skill is English, with `lang="en"`, and the package embeds it at build time. What a module's skill teaches, `teaches`, is in its details, with the same state of the anchor skill. The map does not name the skills that teach an anchor skill, `teaching_skills`: they are full IDs of skills of other modules, and the map does not have their names.
- **Choosing.** On the learner's view, a skill that is "time to review", "mastered", "in progress" or "ready to start" can be chosen, and so can a skill with a rubric or not assessed. For a "locked" skill the map names the prerequisites that are not mastered yet, and it cannot be chosen. `app` passes the chosen skill to the engine, `ChooseNext`; the package gives it out as an event of the element.
- **Two views.** The learner's has the states in words and an icon, not by color alone, and the choice of a skill. The adult's is the same map without the choice, and a skill also has details: the number of pieces of evidence `evidence_count`, the last practice `last_practiced_at`, the mastery band by its title in the module manifest (`bands`) and the review date `review_date` or "time to review". The estimate `estimate`, the uncertainty `uncertainty`, the retention `retention` and the cautious probability `cautious_probability` are shown by neither view. `app` says which view is needed: on the learner's screen the learner's view, in the section for adults the adult's view, and the same for an owner who learns themselves.
- **Without state.** Without an answer of `GetSkillMap` the package draws the graph and the tiers without marks of state and without the choice. This is the map for `site` and the author's tools: they have no learner's data.
- **Data on the device.** No `localStorage`, no IndexedDB, no cache: the state of the skills lives in the element's memory while it is on the page. What the device holds is the register of the device's data of `app`, and for the map it does not change. Computing on the device, for offline, is stage 5 (Т-10).

— *Why:*
- the layout depends on the screen, the zoom and the length of the names in the explanation language, and `api` does not know these. The tier already comes ready, and what is left to the layout is the order of the skills in a tier and the lines of the links — as with MathTrail's map of topics (sources 1.1);
- the product asks the map to show what is mastered, what opens and what it rests on: the graph with the marks "mastered" gives this, and "time to review" comes from the review date in the same answer. The rule "ready to start" is taken from the definition of the code "a new skill" in the contract, so that the map and the engine speak of an open skill as closely as possible;
- a skill opens by its prerequisites: a locked skill is seen on the map but cannot be chosen. The map does not lock a skill on prerequisites whose mastery it cannot see — with a rubric, not assessed, anchor skills —: otherwise a skill with a rubric in a chain would lock everything after it for ever;
- what the answer did not say, the map does not make up: an anchor skill without a record is "unknown", the same reading as for a skill with a verdict that the answer does not name, and "unknown" is the very value as which `progress.proto` says an unfamiliar holding is read. `progress.proto` names which holding each reason explains, and a reason with another holding — for example, with a value that the build reads as "unknown" — would contradict it. The contract does not expect repeats, and the rule of the first record is needed only so that the map on the same data is the same;
- the essay module is in the first slice, and almost all its skills are with a rubric. Without them its map would be empty, and the links through such skills would break;
- the level is per module, and each module has its own scale: the tiers of different modules cannot be compared. `GetSkillMap` gives one module, and modules are linked only by anchor skills — and these are what the map shows;
- the estimate, the uncertainty and the cautious probability are the model's numbers on the scale of the module's bands, and they tell neither the learner nor an adult anything without that scale. The mastery band has its title in the module manifest, and an adult can plan for the review date;
- `canvas` has no data of its own: so here too. Storing the state of the skills on the device would mean a new row in the register of the device's data and a storage period of its own (П-9), and the first slice has no offline.

— *Rejected:*
- coordinates from `module-kit` or `api` as a field of `GetSkillGraph`: the server would know the drawing, and the narrow screen and the zoom would stay with the package anyway;
- the prototype's "new this week" as a field `mastered_at` in `SkillState`: the product does not require it, and a field in `v1` cannot be removed later;
- the state on the map as a field of `api` by the engine's rule: the field would wait for the detailing of `engine`, and the engine takes the map's rule as an input instead, so the two do not part;
- the same map for the learner and the adult, with the details only in the progress of `app`: an adult would look at a skill in two places;
- the model's numbers for the adult too: see "Why";
- hiding the skills with a rubric and those not assessed: see "Why";
- one map of all the connected modules, linked through anchor skills: `app` would call `GetSkillMap` and `GetSkillGraph` for each module, and the tiers of different modules in one drawing would lie about difficulty;
- a cache of the map on the device for showing it without the network: see "Why";
- letting any skill be chosen, with only a warning on a locked one: for a skill whose prerequisites the learner has not mastered, the engine would have to decide what to give, and its rule is not decided yet;
- an anchor skill without a record as "not held": the map would say about the holding what `learner-model` did not say; not showing an anchor skill without a record: the graph names it, and the skill that needs it would lose its link.

— *Consequence:* [architecture.md](architecture.md); [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md).

**R04. The package enters its consumer as one element, `<novatrail-skill-map>`. It takes the graph, the tiers, the module manifest and the state of the skills as properties, the view, the language of the labels and the level of the bands' headings as attributes, and it gives the choice of a skill as the event `skillchoose` with the skill's full ID. It has no methods. On an assignment it updates the map in place, by skill ID. The data types come from the packages of `spec`, `@altnovatrail/spec` and `@altnovatrail/contracts`, and the types of the element itself are its own. The package embeds the English titles of the anchor skills at build time, from the dictionary's schema in the format file of a release of `spec`. Beside the element, the package gives the rule of the states as one function at a second entry point, `./states`, for the module card of `app`; on the page it is still the one element.** How it is built: [architecture.md](architecture.md).
- **Properties** — the data as `app` received them:
  - `graph` — the open view of the skill graph, `open.SkillGraph` from `@altnovatrail/spec/formatv0/open`. `app` reads the bytes of `OpenView` itself, by the format version beside them, and for a version that its build does not read it offers a reload: the element takes no bytes;
  - `tiers` — the tiers `skill_tiers` of the response of `GetSkillGraph`, as they are: `Readonly<Record<string, number>>`, the tier by a skill's local ID;
  - `manifest` — the open view of the module manifest, `open.Manifest`: `id` — for a skill's full ID, `languages.explanation` — for the `lang` of the titles and descriptions of skills, the names of tiers and the titles of difficulty bands, `bands` — the titles of the difficulty bands;
  - `skillMap` — the response of `GetSkillMap`, `GetSkillMapResponse` from `@altnovatrail/contracts`, or `null`. Without it, the map is without state. `app` itself ensures that the graph and the state are of one package: it takes the graph by the `package_digest` of this response.

  The element draws the map when it has `graph`, `tiers` and `manifest`, and follows each assignment by updating it in place, by skill ID: the focus and the expanded skill stay. It does not change the data and keeps them nowhere beyond its memory while it is on the page.
- **Attributes:**
  - `view` — `learner` or `adult`, the learner's view or the adult's view. Without it or with another value, the map is without the choice and without the adult's details: so `app` cannot open the choice by mistake where it is not needed;
  - `lang` — the language of the element's labels. The language is looked up in the table by truncating the tag, and one not found is English. The languages are `en` and `ru`;
  - `heading-level` — the level of the bands' headings, from 2 to 6; without it or with another value, 3. The headings of the bands are parts of the accessibility tree, which is the text alternative of the map, and the level of a heading must match the map's place on the page of `app` (1.3.1 WCAG 2.2, sources 2.1): only the consumer knows it.
- **The event `skillchoose`** — a `CustomEvent` from the host, `bubbles` and `composed`, with a frozen `detail` `{ skill }`: the skill's full ID — the module ID, `skill` and the local ID joined by `/`, as `ChooseNext` expects it in the field `skill`. It comes only when the element has `skillMap` and the learner has chosen, on the learner's view, a skill that is not locked: without state there is no choice. The assignment of properties gives no event.
- **No methods and no other events:** the map has no work to hand over and no state of its own that `app` would keep — unlike `canvas`.
- **Types.** The element's types are in the package's declarations: the element's interface, `SkillChoose` and `SkillChooseEvent`, and the package itself puts the element into `HTMLElementTagNameMap` and its event into `GlobalEventHandlersEventMap`, as `canvas` does. The data types are `@altnovatrail/spec` and `@altnovatrail/contracts` at an exact version, the same as the tag of the submodule `spec`, in the dependency fields of `package.json`.
- **The titles of anchor skills** are the English `title` of the dictionary, `format/schemas/shared-taxonomy.schema.json` in the format file `format-<version>.tar.gz` of a release of `spec`, verified by `release-verify`. The package embeds them at build time — for each format version that its build reads. The ID of an anchor skill that its build does not know it shows as it is, also with `lang="en"`.

— *Why:*
- the platform's rule for its web components: data as properties, changes and requests as events, element names of its own, the types of the element itself from the package, and the data types from the releases of `spec`. The name follows the pattern of `<novatrail-canvas>`;
- parsing the bytes of `OpenView` is already `app`'s job, for the check of the format version, and a second parsing in the element would be a second place of that check;
- the state of the skills is exactly the response that `app` received over Connect: moving it into types of its own would mean a second agreement about the same thing;
- without `view` the choice is closed: an error of the consumer must not let learning begin by an adult's action;
- the update in place — a full redraw would take away the focus and the expansion at each new response of `GetSkillMap` (2.4.3 WCAG 2.2, sources 2.1);
- the package `@altnovatrail/spec` holds only declarations, and the titles of anchor skills are in the dictionary's schema (`spec/tools/types/npm.mjs`; the file `format-<version>.tar.gz` of a release). A release file enters code only through `release-verify`, and the dictionary changes only with the format version, so embedding it at build time is enough.

— *Rejected:*
- an element per skill or per tier: the consumer would assemble the map itself, while the layout is the package's;
- the bytes of `OpenView` as a property of the element: see "Why";
- types of its own for the state of the skills instead of `GetSkillMapResponse`: see "Why";
- the element's types written by hand in `spec`, as for `canvas`: the types come to `app` with the package itself, from one source, and the names of the agreement reach `spec` by the component's card in `spec/docs/architecture.md`;
- the titles of anchor skills from `app`, as a property or a field of `GetSkillMap`: `app` does not have them either, and a field in the contract for a text that changes only with the format version is superfluous;
- the dictionary's schema from the submodule `spec/` at its tag: code takes the files of `spec` from its releases, not from the submodule, and only a release file carries the signature and the attestation that `release-verify` checks;
- the methods `focusSkill()` or `select()`: the consumer does not need them, and the highlight and the focus are held by the layout;
- a fixed level of the headings — on a page where the map stands under a heading of another level, the structure would lie; groups with `aria-label` instead of headings — a screen reader does not jump by them.

— *Consequence:* [architecture.md](architecture.md).

## The layout of the map

**R06. The map is the tiers' bands from top to bottom: before tier 1, the band of anchor skills; the skills in a band wrap, and the page scrolls. The order in a band is by the mean place of the prerequisites. A skill is expanded by a tap, Enter or Space: the details open under it, and lines go only from its prerequisites and to what it opens. Starting a session is the button "Practise" in the details. From the keyboard, Tab through the buttons. Distant tiers do not collapse. The layout is the package's own code, without a library.** How it is built: [architecture.md](architecture.md). The numbers in brackets — 1.4.10 and the like — are the success criteria of WCAG 2.2 (sources 2.1).
- **Bands.** A band is a tier, from top to bottom: first the anchor skills, then tier 1, 2 and on, as many as there are. Only a tier that has skills has a band. A band has a heading: the word for a tier from the package's table and the number, and after them the tier's name from `tiers`, if it has one — "Tier 2 · Sharing", "Tier 3"; the band of anchor skills has its own. The skills of a band are tiles in a row that wraps. The page of `app` scrolls; the map has no scroll container of its own.
- **The order in a band.** Tier 1 — by a skill's local ID. Each next band — by the mean place of a skill's prerequisites among the module's skills, and a place is the number of a tile in its band, from zero; on a tie, by ID. A skill of a tier above 1 without prerequisites among the module's skills stands at the end of its band, by ID. IDs are compared by character codes. Anchor skills give no place, and in their own band they stand by ID. The layout does not rest on the order of the skills in the graph: it has no meaning. The state and the expansion do not change a tile's place.
- **A skill without a tier.** A skill of the graph that has no tier in `skill_tiers` — an integer from 1 — does not stand on the map; nor do the anchor skills needed only by it, and there are no links to it.
- **Anchor skills** in the band are only the prerequisites of the module's skills. If the module has none, there is no band either, with its heading. An anchor skill can be expanded too: the details name its state, its reason and the module's skills that need it.
- **Expansion and details.** A tap, Enter or Space on a tile expands the skill, and a second one closes it; one skill is expanded. Any skill can be expanded, a locked one and an anchor skill too; a skill is chosen for a session only by the button "Practise". The details stand in the DOM right after the tile, as a separate item of the band's list, and take the whole width of the band — the tiles after the expanded one wrap below them. The details hold the title and the description, the state or the mark, what the skill builds on and what it opens, what it teaches, and for the adult also the details of the adult's view. On the learner's view, with `skillMap`, a module's skill that is not locked has the button "Practise", and it gives `skillchoose`, and a locked one has "waits for" and the prerequisites not yet mastered. An anchor skill has no button, nor does any skill on the adult's view, without `view` or with another value of it, or without state. Whatever `ChooseNext` answers — "nothing to give" too, when `api` has limited the pool to tasks without AI — `app` shows: after the event the map waits for nothing. A tile is a disclosure button: `aria-expanded`, and `aria-controls` on the details. The expansion lives in the element's memory and does not go outside.
- **New data.** On the assignment of properties the element updates the tiles in place, by skill ID: the tile that has the focus and the expanded details stay. If the expanded skill is gone with the new data, the details close. If the node that has the focus is gone with the new data — a tile, or the button "Practise" that "waits for" replaced —, the focus moves to the tile of the same skill, and without it to the first tile of the same band — the band of anchor skills or the band of the tier with the same number —, and without it to the first tile of the map.
- **Lines.** Only for the expanded skill: from the tile of each of its prerequisites — a module's skill or an anchor skill — to it, and from it to each skill that it is a prerequisite of. While the details are open, the lines to what the skill opens leave from the bottom edge of the details: the tile and the details are one block, and the lines do not run under them. The package draws the lines by the measured places of the tiles after the layout and draws them again when the size changes. The lines lie under the tiles, and each has a dot at both ends. A link across a tier and a line from an anchor skill pass under the tiles of the bands in between, and a line from the details also under the tiles wrapped below them: what is linked to what is told not by the lines but by the dots at the ends and the labels "needed before" and "opens after" on the linked tiles — only on them. So a link is visible, and not by color alone (1.4.1). The contrast of a line with the background is no less than 3:1 (1.4.11). With `prefers-reduced-motion` the lines appear without animation.
- **A sequential module** follows the same rule: one skill per band. It has no look of its own: the label "sequential" is shown by `app`.
- **Keyboard and focus.** Each tile is an ordinary button in the Tab order: band after band, in the order of the layout. Enter or Space expand the skill, the focus stays on it, and "Practise" is the next Tab stop, because the details stand in the DOM right after the tile. The map has nothing sticky of its own, and `app` answers for the focus not being hidden by something sticky on the page — a header or a bar of `app` (2.4.11).
- **The text alternative** is the DOM tree itself: a band is a heading and a list of tiles, a tile has the accessible name "title, state", and a highlighted one also its label: "title, state, needed before". The label stands inside the tile's button. The links and the details are named in words. The SVG of the lines is `aria-hidden`: everything in it is already said in words. There is no separate list or table. The level of the headings is set by the element's attribute `heading-level`.
- **Targets** are no less than 44 × 44 CSS px. The state on a tile is a word and an icon. At 320 CSS px and at 200% zoom the bands wrap, and nothing scrolls sideways (1.4.10).
- **No collapsing.** All tiers are always visible. Д3 — come back to it when a module has more than six tiers.
- **Own code.** A tier comes ready, and what is left to the layout is the order in a band and the lines of one skill — a few dozen lines of code, not a task for a library.

— *Why:*
- `app` scrolls by the page, and it gives a scroll container of its own only with `tabindex="0"`, the role `region` and a name. Bands that wrap need no sideways scrolling at all: they need no exception of 1.4.10 for diagrams, there is no second direction of scrolling and no need for moving without dragging (2.5.7), and at 200% they simply wrap;
- the order of the DOM and the order on the screen match: band after band, tile after tile, the details after the expanded one (1.3.2, 2.4.3). So the tree is the text alternative, and there is no second representation that could drift from the map;
- with wrapping into several rows, all the lines at once would cross and run through other tiles. The lines of one skill show what the product requires showing — what it builds on and what it opens —, and only when the learner has asked about it. The prototype does so too: the marks "before" and "after" at the chosen topic, without lines (`spec/draft/NovaTrail Widget.html`, `tsteps`);
- `ChooseNext` closes the open attempt, and `app` warns about it: an accidental tap must not start a session. The expansion and the details, and then the button — like the panel "Practise: …" of the prototype. A tablet has no hover, so the highlight follows the expansion, not the hover;
- the mean place of the prerequisites keeps skills near what they build on, in reading order: a skill whose prerequisites are nearer the start of their bands is nearer the start of its own. It stands under its prerequisite on the screen only while the bands do not wrap — as it was in the columns of the MathTrail topic map (sources 1.3). The ID gives the same order on the same data;
- a tier comes with the graph from one package, and the map does not compute it: a skill without a tier is an error of the data, not a reason to invent a place for it. A link leads only to a tile that has a place, otherwise the lines and the labels would have nothing to lead to;
- in the first slice there are no more than four tiers, and up to 17 skills in a module: there is nothing to collapse, and Tab through 17 buttons is shorter than learning a way of moving by arrows of the map's own;
- layout libraries lay the graph out in layers themselves and set absolute coordinates (sources 3.1), while here the layer comes ready, and CSS wraps the tiles.

— *Rejected:*
- all the lines always: with wrapping they tangle;
- columns of tiers from left to right, as in MathTrail, with sideways scrolling in a `region` of its own and buttons for moving instead of dragging: a second direction of scrolling, a container of its own contrary to how `app` scrolls, and a separate text alternative;
- `skillchoose` right on a tap, and the details on hover and focus: an accidental tap would start a session, and a tablet has no hover;
- collapsing distant tiers, as the prototype does: in the first slice there is nothing to collapse, and a collapsed tier is one more state to keep and announce;
- one Tab stop and arrows across the tiles: a way of moving of the map's own, which has to be learned, for graphs of up to 17 skills;
- `@dagrejs/dagre`, `elkjs` and `d3-dag`: see "Why". `elkjs` besides has the license `EPL-2.0 OR GPL-3.0-or-later` (sources 3.1), not a permissive one like Apache-2.0;
- putting a skill without a tier into tier 1: a prerequisite would then be no lower than the skill, while a tier stands above the tiers of its prerequisites; showing an empty band as a heading without tiles.

— *Consequence:* [architecture.md](architecture.md); Д3 in [run/DEBT.md](../run/DEBT.md).

## Ranks

**R07. In the first slice the map shows no ranks: what a rank is in NovaTrail is decided by О-13, and the conclusions of this detailing go there. When ranks come, they will be turned on by an attribute of the element, which `app` sets by the learner's presentation style, on both views of the map; without the attribute there are no ranks.** How it is built: [architecture.md](architecture.md).
- **What the map shows of mastery** — the five states on the map, and for the adult also the mastery band by its title. Neither a skill, nor a module, nor a learner has a rank.
- **What went to О-13** — its row in `spec` holds these conclusions. A learner cannot have a rank at all: the level is per module, and the scales of modules do not add up. Two candidates remain:
  - the rank of a module — the number of the difficulty band in which the module's level stands, "rank k of N", as a number without a name, as in MathTrail (sources 1.2). `GetSkillMap` has no module level, so a field of the contract is needed, and a rule of `learner-model` by which the level falls into a band;
  - the rank of a skill — the number of its mastery band on the module's scale, from what `GetSkillMap` already gives.

  The recommendation is the rank of a module, if О-13 decides that ranks are visible, and until then none at all.
- **Turning on.** Ranks are part of the presentation style, and the map does not know the presentation style and does not read the profile. So an attribute of the element turns them on, and `app` sets it. The attribute's name and the look of a rank on the map will be decided after О-13, in the element's agreement, rewritten in place. How the presentation style holds ranks — as a field of the profile or as a rule by age — is decided by О-13, О-23 and `app`.

— *Why:*
- the level of a module exists only inside `learner-model`, and the estimate of a skill, `estimate`, is the level plus the skill's correction, and no view shows it (`progress.proto` of `spec`): the rank of a module cannot be derived from what the map has, and a field for a rank that О-13 has not decided on yet would be premature;
- the mastery band is the highest of the bands where the skill is taught: a skill taught only in the lowest band would stay "rank 1" forever. Such a rank would speak of the module, not of the learner, and the map does not know the limit: the bands of tasks are closed;
- until the decision of О-13, `app` keeps a neutral tutor without ranks and without a persona: a map with ranks would diverge from the rest of the screen;
- the product requires showing what is mastered, what opens and what it builds on — it does not require a rank, and that much the map already shows;
- the presentation style in the contract so far holds only voicing, and there is no list of styles (О-23): the map cannot decide for `app` what the style consists of.

— *Rejected:*
- the rank of a module as a field of `GetSkillMap` right now: a field in `v1` cannot be removed later, and whether ranks are needed О-13 has not decided yet;
- the rank of a skill by the mastery band: see "Why";
- a common rank of the learner across all modules, as in MathTrail: the scales of modules do not add up;
- ranks always on the adult's view, and by an attribute on the learner's view: the adult sees the mastery band anyway, and two rules of turning on would diverge from the presentation style, of which a profile has one.

— *Consequence:* [architecture.md](architecture.md).

## The element

**R09. The element `<novatrail-skill-map>` builds and updates its tree by its own code, by skill ID: the assignments of one event-loop task merge into one update, a node is moved only when its place has changed, and the focus stays on it or goes by the layout's rule of focus. The words are the package's own table with placeholders, in `en` and `ru`. The English titles of the anchor skills are embedded from the format file of a release of `spec`, which `release-verify` checked once, and from then on its sha256 holds it. The styles are one `CSSStyleSheet` in `adoptedStyleSheets`, and a page under a strict CSP checks them. A trial consumer checks the package's declarations, of both entry points.** How it is built — [architecture.md](architecture.md).
- **The tree** — `src/element/tree.ts`. A band is a `<section>` with a heading of the level `heading-level` and a `<ul>` of tiles. A tile is an `<li>` with a `<button>`: `aria-expanded`, and the expanded one also has `aria-controls` pointing to the details. The details are an `<li id="details">` right after the tile. The nodes of the bands and tiles live in a `Map` keyed by the band and the skill ID, and new data change their text and attributes in place. A node is moved only when its place in the list has changed. If the focus was on it, the focus returns to it. If the node that had the focus is gone, the focus goes to the tile that the core's `focusAfter` names. The button "Practise" stays in the details as long as it is needed — it is not recreated, or the focus would leave it.
- **Assignments** — the setters `graph`, `tiers`, `manifest` and `skillMap` and the changes of the attributes `view`, `lang` and `heading-level` queue one update in a microtask: `app` assigns the data one after another, and the map follows them once. Without `graph`, `tiers` or `manifest` the map is empty. A property that the page assigned before the element was registered lies on it as an own property and hides the setter: the constructor removes it and assigns it again through the setter. Parsing `view` and `heading-level` is in the core, `src/core/attributes.ts`, by the element's agreement.
- **State and choice** — from the core's `mapStates`, `layout` and `linksOf`. "Practise" is there only in the learner's view, with `skillMap`, on a module's skill that is open. It dispatches `skillchoose` from the host — `bubbles`, `composed` and a frozen `detail` `{ skill }`, the full ID for `ChooseNext`. A locked skill has "Waits for" and its prerequisites not mastered, in the order of the layout, as "Builds on" has: [the format text](../spec/format/README.md) gives the graph's order no meaning.
- **The tile's accessible name** is its text: the title, then the state or the mark, then the link's label, separated by commas. The tile's parts are inline, the icon too, an inline SVG beside the word: Chromium surrounds a block or an out-of-flow element with spaces, and the name would become "Quarters , Locked".
- **Words** — `src/element/words.ts`: phrases with something substituted are templates with named places, `{tier}`, `{skills}`, `{band}`. The tree fills them with text or with nodes with their own `lang`, so that a skill's title and a tier's name in a phrase are in the explanation language, and no phrase is glued from pieces; `Intl` and the plural forms are the tree's. The map's container has the `lang` of the words' language, so that a screen reader does not read them by the language of `app`'s page (3.1.2 WCAG 2.2).
- **The titles of the anchor skills** — `src/element/anchor-titles.ts`, which `scripts/taxonomy.ts` writes. The recipe `taxonomy` downloads the format file and its `.sigstore.json` from the release of `spec` that `scripts/taxonomy.ts` names and checks them with `just -f spec/justfile release-verify spec <tag> …`. Then it takes the `title` of each branch of `oneOf` from `format/schemas/shared-taxonomy.schema.json` and writes the table together with the file's sha256. `ci-lint` calls `taxonomy --check`: it downloads the file again, checks the sha256 against the one written in the table, and requires the table to be the one that the script writes from that file. So the release file entered the code through `release-verify`, and from then on the pinned digest holds it. An anchor skill's ID that the table does not know is shown as it is; the title is always with `lang="en"`. Biome does not look at this file: the script sets its bytes, and Biome's format would differ from `--check`. The scripts call `tar` by the path `/usr/bin/tar` of the base image, and `just` by the path that the recipe passes in `JUST`: a program from `PATH` could come from a directory that anyone writes to (SonarCloud `typescript:S4036`).
- **The adult's view** — the numbers of the adult's details only for a skill with a verdict: a skill with a rubric or not assessed has no state in `GetSkillMap`, and "0 answers" would lie. The answer record that the state is taken from — the first, when the answer names a skill twice — the core gives together with the state, in the field `record`, and the element has no second copy of the rule.
- **Styles** — `src/element/styles.ts`: one `CSSStyleSheet`, `replaceSync`, in `shadowRoot.adoptedStyleSheets`, without `<style>` and without the attribute `style`, which a strict CSP refuses. The check is `src/element/csp.test.ts`: an `iframe` with `srcdoc` and `<meta http-equiv="Content-Security-Policy" content="default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self'">` loads a fixture module of the same origin, and it draws the map of `example/fair-shares`. There are no violations, the list has `display: flex`, there is one sheet, and there is no `<style>` and no `[style]`. And an attribute `style` inserted in the same frame is refused as `style-src-attr` — the policy is in force. In Firefox the policy writes the origin out in place of `'self'`. The fixture's imports are static: Vitest rewrites a dynamic `import()` into a call of its runner, which the frame does not have.
- **The trial consumer** — `scripts/consumer.ts` in `ci-test`, after `package.ts`, and on the release's archive in `release-files`, the recipe of the release. It unpacks the archive of `npm pack` into the `node_modules` of a temporary folder, puts beside it links to the repository's `@altnovatrail/spec`, `@altnovatrail/contracts` and `@bufbuild/protobuf`, and checks `scripts/consumer/main.ts` twice with `tsc`: under `NodeNext` and under `Bundler`, with `strict` and without `skipLibCheck`. The element's name gives its type, the properties take the data's types, and `skillchoose` is typed on the element and on the document. A wrong type of `skillMap`, a tier as a string and a write to `detail` stand under `@ts-expect-error`. The element's declarations are `NovatrailSkillMap`, `SkillChoose`, `SkillChooseEvent` and `SkillMapView`, the element in `HTMLElementTagNameMap` and `skillchoose` in `GlobalEventHandlersEventMap`. From `@altnovatrail/skill-map/states` it takes `skillStates` and `SkillStatus`: the function takes the graph, the answer and a time and gives a `ReadonlyMap` of the seven values, refuses `null` for the answer, and a word that is none of the seven is refused as a value.

— *Why:*
- its own tree — the package takes no library of elements, and the map's tree is bands, tiles and one set of details. Updating by ID and moving only what has shifted keep the focus and the expansion, as the element's agreement requires: redrawing would drop them on every answer of `GetSkillMap`;
- one update per event-loop task — four assignments in a row would otherwise build the map four times, and in between on half the data;
- checking the signature once — the shared rules have a release file enter the code through `release-verify`, and from then on be held by its digest, without checking the signature again. `ci-lint` still checks the table against the file: otherwise nobody would notice a title edited by hand;
- the check under CSP in a frame — a page's CSP is set before the page loads, and only in a separate document does the test set the policy that a strict consumer has. The refused attribute `style` shows that the policy in the frame is in force and that a green check means something;
- the trial consumer — the element's types are in its package, not in `spec`, and they can be checked only as a consumer sees them: from the archive, with its own copy of the packages of `spec`, without `skipLibCheck`.

— *Rejected:* `release-verify` on every pull request — the shared rules do not require it; a table of titles without a check in CI; a tile in a column — flex and an out-of-flow element break its accessible name; `aria-label` on the tile — the title would lose its own `lang`; a dynamic `import()` in the CSP fixture — see above.

— *Consequence:* a move to a new tag of `spec` runs `just taxonomy`; `ci-lint` goes to the network for the format file, and a failure of the network there is softened by retrying the download. — `src/core/attributes.ts`, `src/element/`, `scripts/taxonomy.ts`, `scripts/consumer.ts`, `scripts/consumer/main.ts`, `justfile`, `tsconfig.node.json`; [architecture.md](architecture.md).

## Words, icons and look

**R10. The map speaks `en` and `ru`: the language is the element's attribute `lang`, found in the table by truncating the tag, and English when it is not found. The table of words is data alone: templates with places and CLDR's plural forms. A state, a mark and a holding have a Material Symbols icon beside the word, embedded at build time from the set's package, and `NOTICE` names the set; the words and icons of the five states and the two marks are those of the shared tables of `spec`, and a new version of the set comes only by a row of COORDINATION.md of `spec`, together with `canvas` and `app`. The look keeps WCAG 2.2 AA with 2.5.5 and 2.3.3 (sources 2.2): the theme is three CSS variables with light defaults, the targets are from 44 × 44 CSS px, with forced colors there are the system colors, and there is no motion. The tests run axe-core on every view and language.**
- **Language** — the core's `languageOf`, `src/core/attributes.ts`: lookup by truncating the tag, the "Lookup" of RFC 4647, as `canvas`'s `labelsFor`. A singleton subtag — `x` or an extension letter — is cut off together with what follows it; case does not matter. A language not found is `null`, and `wordsFor` takes English, and the `lang` of the map's container is the language of the table found. The element does not read an ancestor's language, as no UI package of NovaTrail does. A new `lang` changes the words in place: the focus and the expansion stay.
- **Words** — `src/element/words.ts`: a `Map` of languages, `en` and `ru`. A phrase with something substituted is a template with the places `{tier}`, `{skills}`, `{count}`, `{date}`; a phrase with a number has the forms of CLDR's categories, and `other` always. The tree takes lists, numbers, forms and days from `Intl` by the table's language, one set per language. ## Lines

**R11. The lines of the expanded skill are cubic curves in one SVG under the map's bands: from the middle of the bottom edge of a prerequisite's tile to the top edge of the expanded tile, and from the bottom edge of its details to the middle of the top edge of each tile that the skill opens. The ends on the expanded tile and under its details are spread across the tile's width. The element measures the lines' places after each render of the tree and again on `ResizeObserver`. There is no animation.** The author's answers of 2026-11-07 in planning T15 (RUN.md, T15, «Решено автором»): a curve, recomputing on render and by the observer, no animation. The rest are the plan's proposals, which the author approved.
- **Geometry** — the core's `linesOf`, `src/core/lines.ts`, without DOM: from the rectangles of the tiles and the details in the map's coordinates, by the links of `linksOf` (T12) in the order of the layout. The curve is `M sx sy C sx my, ex my, ex ey`, where `my` is the middle in height: the line leaves the start and enters the end vertically. The ends on the expanded tile are at the fractions `(i + 1) / (n + 1)` of its width, separately for the lines to it and from it, in the order from left to right in which the other ends stand: so the points do not stick together, and the lines do not cross needlessly — both when the bands wrap and on a right-to-left page. The ends and the curve are accurate to 0.1 CSS px, rounded in one place. A tile without a place gives no line.
- **Drawing** — `src/element/lines.ts`: one `<svg class="lines">`, first in the map's container, `aria-hidden`. Each line has a `<g>` with `data-from` and `data-to`, a `<path>` and two points `<circle r="4">`. The drawing's unit is the map's CSS px: the SVG has no `viewBox`, and the style sets its size. The places are only SVG attributes, `d`, `cx` and `cy`; there is no attribute `style`, and the check under the strict CSP expands a skill and sees two lines without violations (R09). The lines' nodes live by the pair of ends and are moved in place.
- **Under the tiles.** The SVG is absolute over the whole container, `.map` is `position: relative`, and the bands are positioned too and come after it in the DOM: the tiles and the details, with the paper background, lie over the lines, and the lines are visible in the gaps between them. `pointer-events: none` — a touch goes through to the tile.
- **Recomputing.** At its end, `MapTree.render` measures the tiles and the details through `getBoundingClientRect` relative to the map and draws the lines. `ResizeObserver` watches the map, the details and each tile — it takes a tile when the tile is created and lets it go when it is gone — and draws the lines again if a skill is expanded: the page's width, the zoom and a font that finished loading change the tiles' size, and a tile shifted by a neighbour is shifted because the neighbour changed size. The observer does not watch the SVG itself, and drawing the lines does not change the sizes of what is observed — there is no loop. The element got `connectedCallback` and `disconnectedCallback`: removed from the page, it observes nothing, and once back, it observes again, and the observer's first notice draws the lines again.
- **Color** — the theme's accent, `--novatrail-skill-map-accent` (R10): `color` on the SVG, and the line of 2 px and the points are `currentColor`. So an invalid value of the accent gives the lines the ink's color, as it does the tiles, and not an empty `stroke`. The default has 6.7:1 to the paper, and a test holds no less than 3:1 to the map's background (1.4.11). In the forced colors mode — Highlight, as the borders of the linked tiles.
- **No animation** — the lines appear at once, and `prefers-reduced-motion` has nothing to turn off (2.3.3). The item of R10 "the motion to come — the lines of T15 — goes only under `(prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`" is answered by this: no motion came.

— *Why:*
- a curve — as on MathTrail's topic map ([architecture.md](architecture.md), section 4): smooth lines merge less often, and one skill's lines are few;
- the geometry in the core — tests without a browser check it, and the element only measures and draws, as with the layout (R06);
- the observer on the tiles, not only on the map — at the same width of the map, a tile changes size with the font and the language;
- no animation — the link is visible at once, and 2.3.3 asks for neither a test project of its own nor waiting.

— *Rejected:*
- a polyline with right angles — its horizontal segments lie on top of one another in one gap between bands;
- the event of `document.fonts` as well — the observer sees a font finishing loading by the tiles' size;
- a fade-in over 150 ms under `no-preference` — one more test project for motion that the map does not ask for;
- all the ends at the middle of the edge — the points of several lines would merge into one;
- the ends in the order of the layout — when the bands wrap, the lines would cross needlessly.

— *Consequence:* T16 tries the lines by hand on a tablet and on a graph with many tiers; Д3 — collapsing distant tiers — stays.

— `src/core/lines.ts`, `src/element/lines.ts`, `src/element/tree.ts`, `src/element/index.ts`, `src/element/styles.ts`; [architecture.md](architecture.md), sections 3.4 and 3.8; R06, R10; RUN.md, T15.

— *Amendment 2026-11-08:* **a band's heading lies over the lines, as a tile does, but no wider than its text: it has the paper background and `width: fit-content`.** A link across a tier ran over the letters of the heading of the band in between — the headings had no background (Д5 in table 4 of RUN.md, a screenshot of the trial of T16). **R25. - **What the engines found:** WebKit passes every test, forced colours too; Firefox failed three, each by the test and not by the map (measurements 3.1). `lines.test.ts` probed a rounded corner of a tile, which Firefox does not hit, and now probes a line's end under the tile's top edge, with the SVG made to take the pointer for the probe, so that a line over the tile would be hit; `csp.test.ts` wrote `'self'` into the policy of a frame of `srcdoc`, under which Firefox does not run the fixture from the page's origin, and in Firefox alone now writes the origin out, the same policy for a consumer's page served from it; Chromium and WebKit keep `'self'`.
- **The release.** The `justfile` is a path of the release (R13), so the merge gives a release of the package, its archive built from the same code.
